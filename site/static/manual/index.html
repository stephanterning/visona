Manual

Visona manual

Everything you need to install Visona, connect it to your music and read what it shows. Hover over any control in Visona for a tooltip; on a touchscreen, hold a finger on it for half a second.

1FAQ

When I move my track's volume fader, the waveform doesn't change.

Visona sits in an insert slot, and in most DAWs inserts come before the track fader, so Visona sees the signal before the fader. To watch the level after the fader, put the track in a group and Visona on the group, or put Visona on a return or the master.

In Ableton Live the waveform lands a little after the grid.

A plugin with latency, such as a lookahead limiter or a linear-phase EQ, sits before Visona. Live tells every plugin on the track the same playhead, so the audio reaching Visona is late by that latency. Route Visona Sync to Visona's sidechain and Visona measures the latency and moves the audio back onto the grid. See Latency and Visona Sync.

The app shows MIDI CLOCK LOST.

MIDI Clock stopped arriving for more than half a second while the transport ran. Check that your DAW sends MIDI Clock to the port chosen in Settings → MIDI input. To keep watching without clock, press Run free (or Space). See MIDI Clock sync.

macOS says Visona can't be opened.

Builds from 1.0.0 beta 1 on are signed and notarized by Apple, so macOS opens them. An older alpha build is not: remove its quarantine flag with xattr -cr /path/to/Visona.app and open it again. For the plugins, see Installing the plugins.

Ableton Live doesn't list the CLAP plugin.

Live doesn't load CLAP plugins. Use the VST3 or, on macOS, the AU in Live. Reaper and Bitwig Studio load CLAP.

Does Visona change my audio?

No. The plugin is a pass-through effect: the audio goes to the output unchanged while Visona analyzes it. Visona Sync is a separate instrument that only writes a click for Visona's sidechain.

Pinch doesn't zoom on my Raspberry Pi touchscreen, but taps and drags work.

The desktop still turns touches into mouse events. See Touchscreen.

2Introduction

Visona is a beat-synced stereo oscilloscope for music production. It draws your audio as a sweep that runs from left to right across one window of musical time, from a quarter bar to four bars, and starts over at the next window. Because the sweep is locked to the bar, a kick that sits on the beat lands on the same grid line every pass, and you see timing, transients and envelopes in their musical context.

Visona comes in two forms:

  • The standalone app, for macOS on Apple Silicon and for Raspberry Pi. It runs without a DAW or plugin host, on your Mac or as a dedicated display beside your studio gear. It listens to an audio interface and follows MIDI Clock from your DAW, or runs free at a tempo you set. On a Raspberry Pi with a screen it becomes a dedicated studio display.
  • The plugins, VST3, AU and CLAP on macOS and VST3 and CLAP on Windows and Linux. Visona is a pass-through stereo effect that follows the DAW's transport directly. Visona Sync is a companion instrument that measures latency for it.

Visona is free, open-source software under the AGPL-3.0. It is in beta: things can still change from release to release, and your feedback shapes what comes next.

4Installing the app

macOS standalone

The app needs a Mac with Apple Silicon and macOS 14 or later. Unzip Visona-macos-arm64.zip, move Visona.app to Applications and open it. The app is signed and notarized by Apple, so macOS opens it without warnings.

The first time, macOS asks for permission to use the microphone. Visona needs it to read any audio input, including your audio interface.

Standalone on Raspberry Pi

Visona runs on Raspberry Pi OS 64-bit with the desktop, in a release based on Debian 13 (Trixie) or later. Over SSH, this installs the newest release to ~/visona and starts Visona full screen at login:

curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/stephanterning/visona/main/scripts/pi/install-visona.sh | bash

Run the same command again to upgrade; your settings are kept. The Pi setup guide covers flashing the card and setting up the audio interface, and the kiosk guide covers autostart, displays and touchscreens.

For local development on an Apple Silicon Mac, scripts/pi/build-and-deploy-to-pi-over-network.sh user@raspberrypi.local builds the Pi app with Docker, transfers it over SSH and restarts Visona in an existing kiosk installation. See the local build and deployment guide.

On Linux and the Raspberry Pi, a USB audio interface must run in class compliant mode. Use a powered USB hub or the interface's own power supply.

Audio interfaces and MIDI ports can be unplugged and plugged in again while Visona runs: it shows NO AUDIO INPUT while the device is missing and carries on a few seconds after it returns. Visona is developed and tested with an RME Babyface Pro FS, but works with any professional USB audio interface.

Running Visona on a separate device

When Visona runs on a different device from the computer playing the music, such as a Raspberry Pi with its own audio interface, connect the two interfaces digitally if you can: S/PDIF, ADAT or AES/EBU. The samples then arrive unchanged, so 0 dBFS in your DAW is 0 dBFS in Visona and the levels you read are exact. Run both interfaces at the same sample rate, with the receiving interface clocked from its digital input.

With an analog connection, match the levels so that 0 dBFS is the same on both sides: the output level of the interface on the computer and the input gain of the interface Visona listens to. Play a sine tone at −12 dBFS from your DAW, open the diagnostics overlay (D) and set the input gain until each input reads −12 dBFS. If the levels don't match, the waveform and its dBFS reference lines show the audio louder or quieter than it is, and too much gain clips the input before Visona sees it.

7Using the plugin in a DAW

  1. Insert Visona on a stereo track, group, return or the master: wherever you want to see the audio.
  2. Press Play. The sweep starts at bar 1 and follows the DAW's tempo, position and time signature.
  3. Stop, and the view freezes and dims with STOPPED in the status bar. Move the playhead and play again, and the scope picks up at the new position.

Use as many instances as you like. Each one saves its own analysis view and view settings, window, gain, auto gain, afterglow, waveform mode and colour in the project. The plugin has no tempo control, pause button or full-screen button, since the DAW drives it; Settings holds the waveform colour only. Until the DAW plays, the state reads STOPPED, also right after the plugin is loaded.

8The screen at a glance

  • Status bar, at the top: tempo, sync state, sample rate, window, gain and zoom.
  • Zoom strip, under the status bar, only while you are zoomed in.
  • Analysis view: choose Scope, Spectrum (FFT), Spectrum (Band) or Stereo from the control bar. Scope has one lane per channel, left above right, with the bar grid and the green write head.
  • Control bar, at the bottom: the view selector, Pause, WINDOW, BPM, GAIN with AUTO, WAVE, then Diagnostics, Full screen and Settings. In the other views WINDOW, BPM and WAVE make way for Afterglow and the view's own button: Analysis settings in Spectrum (FFT) and Reset view in Stereo.

The buttons show only their icons; hover over one, or hold a finger on it, for its name. The control bar stays on one row and wraps onto a second only in a narrow desktop window. In a very small window, Diagnostics and Full screen move into Settings.

10MIDI Clock sync

The app follows MIDI Clock, including Start, Stop, Continue and Song Position Pointer, so it starts at the right bar when you start playback from the middle of a song. In your DAW, send MIDI Clock to a port Visona can see: an output of your audio or MIDI interface, a network MIDI session, or on macOS an IAC bus. Choose that port under Settings → MIDI input.

In Ableton Live, turn on Sync for that output port under Settings → Link, Tempo & MIDI, and set MIDI Clock Type to Song so Live sends Song Position Pointer. The app counts in 4/4.

If clock stops for more than half a second while the transport runs, the state reads MIDI CLOCK LOST and the view freezes until clock returns. The diagnostics overlay shows the MIDI input, the transport, the tempo and the message counts.

11FREE mode and Run free

Without MIDI Clock the app runs free: the sweep moves at the tempo in the BPM control, and the state reads FREE. The app starts in FREE mode and switches to MIDI Clock as soon as clock arrives, so with None as MIDI input it simply stays free.

When MIDI Clock stops or is lost, the first button in the control bar becomes Run free. Press it, or Space, and the sweep carries on at the last clock tempo until MIDI Clock starts again.

12Pause

In the app, Pause (Space or P) holds the view and stops the analysis: use it to study a pass in peace, when movement distracts, or to save CPU. The button turns into a lit Resume, and the state reads PAUSED. While paused, error banners stay hidden; they show again when you resume.

The plugin has no pause button: its view freezes whenever the DAW stops.

13Window

WINDOW sets how much musical time one pass of the sweep covers: ¼, ½, 1, 2 or 4 bars. Keys 1 to 5 pick them directly. A short window shows the shape of a single hit; a long window shows a whole phrase and how its dynamics develop.

14Tempo

The BPM control sets the tempo of the free-running sweep, from 40 to 300 BPM. The − and + buttons step whole BPM; drag or scroll on the value to fine-tune in steps of 0.1. While MIDI Clock sets the tempo, the control shows it dimmed. The free tempo is saved.

15Gain and auto gain

GAIN and AUTO are shared by all visualizations and always appear in the bottom control bar. Gain ranges from 0 to +64 dB in 1 dB steps. It scales waveform amplitudes and stereo radii, and moves both FFT curves and Band bars and markers by the same number of dB. Spectrum scales continue to label actual signal levels: at +6 dB gain the top is −6 dBFS. Gain affects presentation only, never audio, correlation or signal validity. Drag or scroll on it, use its buttons, or use +/− and ↑/↓ in the standalone app. Double-click or double-tap resets it to 0 dB.

AUTO uses the same incoming channel peaks in every view, whether or not MIDI Clock or the DAW is playing. It lowers gain immediately when a peak would exceed the display, and once peaks have stayed low for 10 seconds it raises it, in 3 dB steps, as far as the loudest of them allows, up to +63 dB. Silence below the detection threshold does not cause gain to rise. Switching views does not restart Auto. After a UI interruption long enough to lose the retained peak history, Auto keeps its current gain and waits for a new ten-second history before raising it. Current peaks can still lower gain immediately. Setting gain manually turns Auto off; switching Auto off retains its current gain. Auto follows the audio level, so each individual spectrum curve or stereo profile need not fill the plot.

Afterglow is shared by FFT, Band and Stereo and hidden in Scope. Select Off, 0.5 s, 1 s (default) or 2 s. For FFT it sets the time for the white history line to fall 12 dB, without a hold: 0.5 s gives 24 dB/s, 1 s gives 12 dB/s, and 2 s gives 6 dB/s. For Band it sets peak-marker hold time, followed by a fall of 12 dB/s; an equal or higher peak restarts the hold. In Stereo it sets the fade to 1% brightness for sample points or 1% radius for Polar Level. Off hides the FFT history line and Band markers and removes stereo display history. The shared gain and afterglow are remembered in the app and per plugin instance.

In Scope, faint reference lines mark signal levels every 3 dB after gain, without a fixed lower dB limit. Right-hand labels continue as far as screen space allows; lines continue to pixel resolution near the zero-amplitude centre. A peak beyond a lane is cut off with a grey display-overshoot marker. In Stereo, retained visible history follows gain changes; history moved outside the plot may be discarded.

16Spectrum and analysis views

The view selector at the left of the control bar switches between Scope, Spectrum (FFT), Spectrum (Band) and Stereo. On desktop it opens a compact list; on touch and in kiosk mode it opens large tiles. Press V to open the selector, or Alt+1 / Alt+2 / Alt+3 / Alt+4 to select a view when the app or plugin editor has focus. The app remembers your choice; plugins save it per instance. The window keys 1–5 and W work in Scope only.

The spectrum and stereo views come after 1.0.0 beta 1. They are on the main branch and will be in the next release; until then they need a build of your own from main, since no published development build has them yet.

Both spectrum views use a black background. Spectrum (FFT) shows incoming audio on a logarithmic frequency axis. Its defaults are a fast peak curve with a solid fill in the selected waveform colour and a slow white peak line, both smoothed over 1/12 octave. It follows audio even when MIDI Clock or host playback is stopped. In the standalone app, Pause holds the spectrum and stops its analysis.

Analysis settings contains Channels, Range, Slope, one shared Detector (Peak or RMS) and one shared Smoothing setting (Raw or 1/24–1 octave). Both curves use the same detector and smoothing. The filled curve always uses Fast (150 ms) response and Solid drawing in the selected waveform colour. The second curve is a white history line controlled by global Afterglow. There are no per-curve visibility, response, style or colour controls, and no Reset peaks button.

For the filled curve, Peak follows rises immediately and uses a 150 ms release time constant; RMS averages power with a 150 ms time constant. The history line follows new per-frame levels immediately and falls immediately without a hold, taking the selected Afterglow duration to fall 12 dB. RMS detection scales its per-frame powers consistently before the history envelope; it is not a second slow power average. The FFT window also smooths changes over time. While Analysis settings covers the plot, curve drawing is suspended; close it with Done to see the result.

Channels are Stereo average, Left, Right, Mid and Side. Stereo average combines power without phase cancellation; Mid and Side use (L+R)/2 and (L-R)/2. Global Gain replaces Top; Range sets the visible dB span. Slope adds 0–6 dB per octave around 1 kHz for display only; its default is 3 dB/octave. Pointer and touch readouts show frequency and curve levels, including slope when selected, without adding display gain.

Spectrum (Band) uses 31 streaming bandpass filters at nominal third-octave centres from 20 Hz to 20 kHz, with no FFT window. Bars follow filtered stereo energy with fast attack and a 150 ms peak-release time constant. White peak markers hold for the global Afterglow duration, then fall at 12 dB/s; Off hides them. The bar colour follows Waveform colour. Scope/FFT-specific controls do not change this view; its visible span is 60 dB, positioned by global Gain and it has no display slope.

Band responds quickly in the mids and highs; narrow bass filters still take time to settle. It shows energy in broad bands, so it is intended for musical balance and motion rather than separating close tones. A band is omitted if its upper edge exceeds Nyquist. Levels are peak-calibrated for a centre-frequency sine, with stereo energy averaging that does not cancel opposite-phase channels. This is not an SPL or standards-certified octave-band meter. Only the selected spectrum analysis runs. The standalone Pause button holds either spectrum view.

Peak is calibrated to a sine's peak amplitude. RMS is an average of spectral power and reads 3.01 dB lower for a steady sine before smoothing. Octave smoothing averages power and reduces narrow tone peaks. These are FFT measurements, not calibrated SPL or LUFS. The fixed 8,192-sample window gives 5.86 Hz bin spacing and takes 171 ms to fill at 48 kHz; smoothing cannot add frequency resolution. The display ends at 20 kHz or Nyquist, whichever is lower.

17Stereo analysis

Select Stereo from the view selector or press Alt+4. Switch between Polar Sample, Polar Level and Lissajous with the buttons above the plot. All use the same 100 ms of incoming audio; correlation, balance and signal validity use a 200 ms window, with analysis snapshots published 120 times per second and the display targeting 60 frames per second. Stereo follows incoming audio even when transport is stopped.

Polar Sample folds the sample directions into a semicircle. Mono is vertical, a single channel lies on its diagonal, and opposite-polarity signals extend towards the horizontal edges. Lissajous keeps the signed channel geometry: mono is vertical, opposite polarity is horizontal, and a sine with a 90-degree phase difference makes a circle. These two are sample-density views.

Polar Level offers two profiles over the same 100 ms window. Directional level (default) shows RMS per direction, with samples shared linearly between adjacent 2.5-degree centres. Directions with less than 1% of a window's weighted sample support are attenuated to reduce isolated side spikes. Smooth, bounded transitions join the centres. Energy contribution retains the original calculation: each direction's squared radii are divided by the entire window's sample count, so spreading energy across more directions lowers individual lobes. In both profiles the fill shows RMS and the outline shows the maximum sample radius within angular support; radius is the larger absolute channel sample. Afterglow adds display release. Switching profiles clears history and retains gain and afterglow for comparison. Neither profile claims to reproduce Ozone's unpublished calculation exactly.

Correlation ranges from +1 for proportional signals of the same polarity to -1 for opposite polarity. Near zero can indicate decorrelation or a quadrature relationship. Balance compares channel energy: -1 is left, +1 is right, and 0 is equal energy. It does not measure stereo width. Use the views together with listening, including a mono check.

Each channel becomes measurable at -90 dBFS RMS and remains measurable until it falls below -96 dBFS RMS. At silence or very low levels the panel shows No measurable signal and hides the values; previous polar history fades according to Afterglow. If only one channel is measurable, its cloud or level profile and balance remain visible, but correlation shows --. A new stream or an audio gap requires a fresh complete window. Invalid samples suppress measurements; a stream that stops delivering snapshots changes to Waiting for audio.

Display gain applies equally to both channels and affects only the drawing. It never changes correlation, balance, the signal threshold or the audio. If the drawing exceeds its scale, Display clipped appears; reduce display gain. Polar Level caps the drawn profile at the boundary while retaining the uncapped calculation; sample views omit out-of-scale points. Afterglow selects Off, 0.5 s, 1 s (default), or 2 s: the time for sample brightness or Level radius to decay to 1%. Polar Sample and Lissajous retain fading points; Polar Level responds immediately to increases and releases decreases. Off removes display history in every mode. History may fade during silence while readouts remain invalid; invalid audio, missing streams and view changes clear history. Reset view restores Polar Sample and the default Level profile while retaining global Gain and Afterglow. The colour follows Waveform colour. Mode, Level profile, display gain and afterglow are remembered in the app and per plugin instance. On Retina and other high-density displays the stereo plot uses physical display resolution for finer sample points and sharp contours. Moving the window between display scales clears afterglow history. The standalone Pause button holds the view and stops its analysis.

18Waveform modes

WAVE, or the W key, switches between three ways of drawing:

STD
A thin line through the signal. The lightest to draw, and the clearest for low frequencies and single cycles.
PRECISE
Fills every peak, so nothing between two pixels is lost. The default, and the best view of transients and envelopes.
DJ
Like PRECISE, with every column coloured by frequency: lows red, mids green and highs blue, mixed so the strongest band sets the hue. Kicks and bass glow red, hi-hats blue. The bands split at about 200 Hz and 2.5 kHz.

The outline of the waveform is the same in PRECISE and DJ; only the colour differs.

19Waveform colour

In Settings, choose the colour of the waveform in STD and PRECISE, the spectrum fill, the band bars and the stereo views: Teal (the default), Cyan, Blue, Violet, Pink, Amber, Yellow or Grey. Green is kept for the write head. In the plugin, each instance remembers its own colour, so you can tell instances apart at a glance.

20Grid, lanes and labels

  • Bar lines are bright, beat lines darker, and finer subdivisions fainter still. The grid gets finer as you zoom in, down to sixty-fourth notes.
  • Bar numbers run along the bottom. Zoomed in, the labels read bar.beat, and the left edge always says which beat the view starts in.
  • The grid resolution, in the bottom-right corner, reads like 1/16 · 125 ms: the note value between the finest lines and its length at the current tempo.
  • Lanes are labelled L and R, with faint reference lines labelled in dBFS.
  • The write head is the green line where new audio is drawn. A small gap in front of it erases the previous pass.

21Zoom

Zoom in on any part of the window to see detail, while the sweep keeps running:

  • Drag across the scope to zoom to that part.
  • Scroll up or down to zoom around the pointer, or pinch on a trackpad or touchscreen.
  • Scroll sideways, or hold Shift and scroll, to move the view.
  • Double-click the scope, press Esc, or click the × on the zoom strip to reset.

While zoomed, the zoom strip shows the whole window, the part in view and the write head. Drag on it to move the view, round past either end, or click outside the part in view to centre it there.

22Measurement ruler

Drag with the right mouse button, a two-finger click or Control-click, or hold a finger still for half a second and then drag, to draw a ruler on the scope. While you hold it, a readout next to the pointer shows the ruler's width as:

msThe length in milliseconds.
samplesThe length in samples at the current sample rate.
frequencyThe frequency whose period is that length, in Hz.
noteThe nearest note and how many cents off it is, such as A3 +0 ct. Middle C is C3, as in Ableton Live.
lengthThe musical length, such as 0.75 beat · 3/16 or 4.00 beats · 1 bar.

Measure from one peak to the next to find the pitch of a bass note, or from the grid to a transient to see how late it is. The ruler reads the time axis as shown and goes away when you let go.

23Latency and Visona Sync

Some DAWs, Ableton Live among them, report the same playhead to every plugin on a track, including plugins placed after plugins with latency. The audio reaching Visona is then late by that latency, and the waveform lands after the grid. Visona Sync fixes this.

Visona Sync is an instrument that writes a short impulse at the start of every bar. The DAW aligns a sidechain with the main input of the plugin it feeds, so the impulse arrives exactly as late as the audio. Visona measures how far after the bar line it arrives, and draws the audio that much earlier.

  1. Add Visona Sync on an instrument track. No MIDI clip is needed.
  2. In Visona, choose the Sync track as the sidechain input. In Live, pick Post FX.
  3. Press Play. From the first bar line the status bar shows the measured latency, such as SC +98.7 ms; SC ... means the sidechain is on but no impulse has arrived yet.

Visona keeps the latency it measured. If you add or remove plugins with latency, the new value takes over after two bars and the sweep starts over. One Visona Sync track can feed every Visona instance in the project.

Reaper: set Visona's track to 4 channels and send the Sync track to channels 3/4. Bitwig Studio: choose the Sync track as the sidechain in Visona's device header.

Route nothing but Visona Sync to the sidechain: any peak above −20 dBFS counts as a bar impulse. If Reduced Latency When Monitoring is on in Live, keep the Sync track unarmed.

24Visona Sync with the app

The app allows for its own input latency, but not for what lies between your DAW's MIDI Clock and the audio reaching the interface: the DAW's output latency, clock delay settings and the interfaces on the way. Visona Sync measures that too, if its impulse comes in on an input of its own. This alignment correction does not affect MIDI Clock loss detection: the warning appears only when clock pulses stop arriving for more than half a second.

  1. In your DAW, add Visona Sync on an instrument track and send that track straight to a free output of your interface, such as 3/4, not to the master. Keep it off your speakers: it is a click on every bar.
  2. Connect that output to a free input of the interface Visona listens to, with a cable or the interface's loopback routing (TotalMix on an RME interface, for example).
  3. In Visona's Settings, set Visona Sync to that input.
  4. Start playback with MIDI Clock going to Visona. From the first bar line the status bar shows the offset, such as SC +12.3 ms.

The impulse must reach the input exactly as Visona Sync writes it. Put no effects after Visona Sync on its track, and don't route the track through the master, a group or a return: effects there can delay, smear or turn down the click, and on the master it would also be mixed into your music. Leave the track's volume at 0 dB, since a peak below −20 dBFS doesn't count as an impulse.

Ableton Live: on the Sync track, set Audio To to Ext. Out and choose the output pair below it, such as 3/4. Reaper: in the Sync track's routing, turn off Master send and add a hardware output to the free pair. Bitwig Studio: set the Sync track's output to an output bus for the free pair, added under Settings → Audio.

Nothing is measured in FREE mode. The song must be in 4/4, and the offset must be less than half a bar.

25Diagnostics overlay

Press D, or the Diagnostics button, for a panel of technical readings: the audio device, sample rate, block size and level per input, overruns and device xruns, MIDI Clock messages and timing, the Visona Sync measurement, analysis load, frame rate and render times, and Visona's CPU use. Frame rate and render times follow the selected analysis view. Display refresh rate, full redraws and tile counts appear only for Scope; Stereo reports its physical display scale and timing maxima for the latest reporting interval. Stereo aims at 60 frames per second or more: Polar Sample polls at 240 Hz and Polar Level at 120 Hz to allow for timer delays, and only fresh 120 Hz analysis snapshots produce frames. Band targets 30, and FFT 30 (25 on ARM64 Linux); both slow down on slower hardware to keep the controls responsive. Include a screenshot of it when you report a problem. For Raspberry Pi development, the local deployment script accepts --profile to log FPS, render/paint times and CPU over SSH even with this overlay closed; see the performance measurement guide.

26Full screen and kiosk mode

In the app, F or the Full screen button toggles full screen. On a Raspberry Pi installed with install-visona.sh, Visona starts full screen at login in kiosk mode, with the control bar drawn larger for touch. The display can be switched on after boot, turned off and on, or changed to another resolution, and Visona follows. To quit kiosk mode from SSH, run pkill Visona.

27Touchscreen

Visona works with a multitouch screen, for example on a Raspberry Pi:

  • A tap works as a click. Double-tap the gain to reset it, or the scope to reset the zoom.
  • Tap the view selector for large tiles of the analysis views.
  • Drag across the scope to zoom to that part; pinch to zoom and move the view with your fingers.
  • Hold a finger still on the scope for half a second, then drag, to draw the measurement ruler.
  • Hold a finger still on a control for half a second to see its tooltip.
  • Tap outside the settings panel to close it.

On Raspberry Pi OS, install-visona.sh turns off the desktop's touch-to-mouse emulation so pinch reaches Visona; it takes effect after a reboot. See the kiosk guide for details.

29Troubleshooting

ProblemWhat to try
NO AUDIO INPUTChoose the audio device in Settings. On macOS, allow microphone access under System Settings → Privacy & Security → Microphone. On Linux and the Pi, put the interface in class compliant mode.
The scope runs but stays flatCheck Left input and Right input in Settings, and the input levels in the diagnostics overlay.
MIDI CLOCK LOST or no tempoCheck that the DAW sends MIDI Clock to the chosen MIDI input, and that the DAW is playing.
The waveform is off the gridIn a DAW, use Visona Sync on the sidechain. With the app, use Visona Sync on an input.
The plugin doesn't show upCheck the install folder, unblock the zip on Windows, and rescan in the DAW. An alpha build on macOS also needs its quarantine flag cleared with xattr -cr and an ad-hoc signature with codesign --force --sign -.
The scope stops updating in a DAWNote the status bar and the diagnostics overlay, then close and reopen the plugin window. Report it with those details.
